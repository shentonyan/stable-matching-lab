# Study scarce information before recommending matches

Stable Matching Lab becomes more useful for research by making **information, inspection capacity and conflicting objectives** explicit. The central question is: under a fixed bilateral inspection budget and a shared signal model, when does targeting predicted compatibility improve cost-adjusted population surplus, and when do congestion and stable reassignment reverse that gain? This question connects the simulator to established work on costly preference discovery and matching congestion. The project now separates mathematical statements, synthetic comparisons and an observed-data audit. These layers support reproducible investigation, but **do not establish novelty, a strategic equilibrium or calibrated real-world superiority**. Public dating data provide a descriptive reality check, not the missing identification of utility or policy effects.

## Scarce meetings connect the project to established theory

Costly encounters are a substantive modeling choice with close predecessors. Rastegari, Goldberg and Manlove study interviews that reveal information to both participants and show general computational difficulty in minimizing interviews, alongside tractable restricted settings. The repository therefore cannot claim that costly bilateral learning is new; its contribution at this stage is an inspectable experimental implementation and comparison framework ([2016 author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.04792)). Immorlica, Leshno, Lo and Lucier connect costly information acquisition to market-clearing cutoffs and regret-free stability in college admissions. Their information-deadlock result makes it especially important to distinguish a numerical reservation-value closure from an equilibrium guarantee ([author manuscript, 16 June 2020](https://immorlica.com/pubs/infoAcq.pdf)).

The distinction has empirical relevance. Hakimov, Kübler and Pan compare direct and sequential serial dictatorship theoretically and experimentally, finding benefits when sequential information directs search toward feasible options. Their exam-priority setting differs from bilateral dating preferences; their welfare conclusions cannot simply be imported into this simulator ([Quantitative Economics 14(4), 2023, 1447–1490; DOI 10.3982/QE1955](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.3982/QE1955)). Ashlagi, Braverman, Kanoria and Shi study signals and recommendations that reduce the communication needed to reach stable outcomes under specified preference assumptions. Their communication-based congestion is a close conceptual predecessor, but differs from a hard inspection-degree cap or a utility penalty for overload ([Management Science, 2019; DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2018.3265](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/mnsc.2018.3265)).

The research comparison should consequently hold latent worlds, observable signals and budget definitions fixed. Actual inspections and person-level exposure must accompany nominal caps. Side-specific outcomes matter because equal match counts do not imply equal utilities. The local arguments in [theory.md](theory.md) establish restricted revealed stability and a planner bound, and give counterexamples to universal welfare or match-count monotonicity as information grows. They are not claims of new theorems. The original pooled cutoff remains an accounting closure without a proof of strategic best responses, existence and uniqueness; [model-audit.md](model-audit.md) records this boundary.

## Stability and fair exposure measure different outcomes

**Stability must name its information domain.** Aziz, Biró, Gaspers, de Haan, Mattei and Rastegari analyze uncertain preference models and show that stability questions depend on the representation of uncertainty. In this repository, no blocking pair among inspected edges does not imply no blocking pair among all latent pairs ([2016 author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.02917)). A synthetic analyst can evaluate hidden blocking because it knows latent utilities. An empirical analyst with unobserved pairs cannot report the same quantity without further assumptions.

Exposure equality is a separate objective. Patro, Biswas, Ganguly, Gummadi and Chakraborty's FairRec formalizes producer exposure and customer fairness within a particular allocation model. Those guarantees do not automatically apply to an exposure-balancing scheduling heuristic here ([The Web Conference 2020; DOI 10.1145/3366423.3380196](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10764)). Report exposure dispersion, side means, lower-tail outcomes and explicit group gaps alongside net surplus and match coverage. None substitutes for the others. Aggregate cardinal surplus also requires a declared utility scale; nontransferable utilities do not become transferable simply because an analyst sums them.

## A real dating service motivates the setting without validating it

Huang, Burtch, He and Hong provide a randomized field experiment on a mobile dating platform. Demand disclosure was particularly effective when paired with a message emphasizing capacity constraints, and effects differed across users. This supports investigating attention congestion and heterogeneity, but does not estimate this project's overload penalty or the effect of its meeting scheduler ([Information Systems Research, 2022; DOI 10.1287/isre.2022.1148](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/isre.2022.1148)).

The official FirstDate page describes questionnaire-based Gale–Shapley matching, one introduction per cycle and contact exchange following mutual acceptance. It explicitly distinguishes compatibility from chemistry. This is a genuine institutional motivation, with no implication that the repository reproduces FirstDate's private implementation, participant behavior or effectiveness ([official pilot website, checked 21 October 2026](https://firstdate.sandbox.gov.sg/)). No public technical evaluation or outcome dataset for that service was verified. Claims about improved relationships, marriage or social welfare remain unsupported.

## Observed choices provide a limited empirical bridge

Fisman, Iyengar, Kamenica and Simonson observe individual accept/reject choices following four-minute encounters among Columbia graduate and professional students. The paper uses **14 sessions**, excluding seven others, including intervention sessions. The public all-waves file is therefore not automatically their estimation sample ([Quarterly Journal of Economics 121(2), 2006, 673–697; DOI 10.1162/qjec.2006.121.2.673](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/121/2/673/1884033); [original paper and experimental design](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/867/fisman%20iyengar.pdf)).

The original [CSV](https://www.stat.columbia.edu/~gelman/arm/examples/speed.dating/Speed%20Dating%20Data.csv) and [Word dictionary](https://www.stat.columbia.edu/~gelman/arm/examples/speed.dating/Speed%20Dating%20Data%20Key.doc) were reachable on 21 October 2026. The dictionary was downloaded and text-extracted locally: it confirms participant and partner IDs, female=0/male=1, binary match coding, and scorecards completed after each date. It also documents design differences: wave 5 undergraduates, wave 12 limited yes decisions to half of encountered people, waves 13–14 a different master of ceremonies, and waves 18–21 book/magazine activities. **Wave 12 limits acceptances, not inspections**, and does not identify the effect of this project's scheduling policy. The local parser retains wave and pair identity, checks mirrored decisions and reports exclusions. Its saved [aggregate audit](../results/empirical_audit.json) contains **8,378 directed rows, 4,184 usable encounters, 690 mutual-yes encounters and 21 waves**. These are this implementation's descriptive results, not a published estimate or a causal effect; [data-protocol.md](data-protocol.md) specifies the denominator and limitations.

The next empirical step is to validate a pre-encounter binary acceptance model using documented questionnaire timing, session-held-out evaluation and calibration diagnostics. Post-date ratings such as attractiveness and liking must be excluded from an ex-ante exposure model. Retrospective observed-edge analyses can use them if labeled accordingly. An observed mutual-yes maximum matching is only a combinatorial ceiling under an imposed one-to-one constraint: participants' binary choices supply neither strict rankings nor cardinal utilities.

Modern platform validation would additionally require exposure logs, reliable outcome definitions and either randomized scheduling or defensible exposure propensities. Missing encounters cannot become negative labels. Costs, discount factors, outside values and long-term utility remain unidentified from these data. The public sample also does not represent modern app users. Keep the original data local; no explicit redistribution license for the original file was verified.

## Evidence determines the next research claim

A useful near-term result is a conditional mechanism comparison: identify parameter regions where gains survive matched budgets, common information and independent evaluation worlds, and document regions where they fail. A broader theoretical or real-world claim requires additional proofs or identification. The strongest research artifact is thus a transparent path from assumptions to falsifiable comparisons, with clear boundaries around what observed choices can resolve.
